## 0. Identity and the binding directive

**Lacteva is the flagship product of Phoenix Software.** This repository is not a standalone application, and the difference is load-bearing: a standalone application may serve the customer in front of it, while a flagship product must serve customers who have not arrived yet, on deployments nobody has provisioned, in countries nobody has sold into.

Lacteva must serve **six operating scales in one architecture** — individual farmers, collection centers, dairy cooperatives, private dairies, milk unions, and multi-country deployments. Not segments to choose between: a design that serves one by making another impossible has failed, even if it ships. The architecture must support **years of evolution** — today's module boundaries are tomorrow's service seams, and today's schema is restored from a backup taken by a version nobody runs any more.

[**STD-0007 — Phoenix Software Engineering Standard**](../00-standards/STD-0007-phoenix-software-engineering-standard.md) is a permanent directive, not a work order. It has no scope and no completion, and it outlives whatever work order is in flight. Its fifteen principles, in short:

1. Architecture before implementation · 2. Business rules before code · 3. Security by default · 4. Multi-tenancy by default · 5. Offline-first where applicable · 6. **Every production guarantee must be executable** · 7. No feature without automated tests · 8. No feature without observability · 9. No duplicated concepts · 10. Reusable platform capabilities over feature-specific implementations · 11. Maintainability over cleverness · 12. Every architectural shortcut documented · 13. Protect backwards compatibility whenever practical · 14. Minimize technical debt · 15. Understandable by a new senior engineer six months from now.

**Principle 6 is the one with evidence behind it in this repository.** VER-001 and DR-001 did nothing but execute guarantees that were already written, already reviewed and already CI-wired, and found **nine defects** — four of them fatal. The platform could not serve a single request on PostgreSQL; every RLS policy was inert because the application connected as a superuser; every backup aborted on a `time` column; and a corrupt backup restored silently, turning a settlement worth 5647.50 into one worth 1.00. None was findable by reading. So: **a PostgreSQL guarantee tested only on SQLite is untested**, a skipped proof is worse than an absent one because it is green, and a proof must show the guard is *capable of refusing* rather than merely present.

**Principle 10** means preferring the Notification Engine to an SMS implementation, the Payment Framework to a Razorpay implementation, the Authentication Platform to a login endpoint, the Reporting Engine to one report. The test before implementing: what does the *second* instance look like — and if allowing for it means touching the first, the seam is wrong. The counterweight is principle 11: a capability with one implementation and no second in sight is speculative generality.

**Review policy.** Future work orders should keep challenging previous assumptions, including this file's. When an earlier decision is *objectively* incorrect — factually wrong, in violation of a stated principle, or contradicted by executing it — document it, correct it, update the documentation, and **never preserve a mistake merely because it already exists.** The bar is deliberately above "I would have done it differently"; DBR-001 raised two findings that ABR-002 correctly withdrew, and recording that mattered as much as the findings that stood.

**Definition of Done** — no work order is complete until all six are *considered*, with the conclusion stated: implementation, tests, documentation, architecture consistency, operational impact, production readiness. "Considered and not applicable, because …" is a complete answer; silence is not.

## 1. Vision

**Lacteva is an AI-first, cloud-native, multi-tenant Dairy Intelligence Platform.** The long-term mission is to digitize the entire dairy value chain — from the farmer pouring milk at a village collection center to processing, settlement, and market intelligence — for **1,000,000+ dairy businesses across 50+ countries**.

**Why it exists.** Dairy in most of the world runs on paper registers, disconnected spreadsheets, trust-based measurement, and month-end settlement disputes. Milk is perishable, quality-priced, and collected twice daily from thousands of small suppliers — a domain where digitization compounds: accurate weights and quality readings feed fair pricing, fair pricing feeds farmer trust and retention, and the resulting data feeds credit, breeding, nutrition, and market decisions no participant can make today.

**Customers.** Dairy cooperatives and their unions, private chilling-center networks, mid-size dairies and processors, and (later) enterprise mega-dairies. The paying customer is the *organization*; the daily users are collection-center operators, field staff, quality labs, accountants, and managers. Farmers/suppliers are first-class subjects of the system before they are users of it.

**Problems solved (in build order).** Trustworthy milk collection (identity, weight, quality, immutable transaction records) → transparent quality-based pricing → supplier settlement → operations intelligence → the wider capability universe documented in the [Business Capability Model](../05-capabilities/CAP-0001-business-capability-master-map.md): **10 domains, ~40 subdomains, 86 capabilities** — the authoritative map of everything Lacteva may ever build.

**Short-term goal.** A production-grade procurement core: collection centers, suppliers, operational readiness, the milk collection transaction engine, and the Pricing Platform — on a platform foundation (identity, tenancy, RBAC, events, audit) that was deliberately built *before* any dairy logic.

## 2. Product Philosophy

- **Business capabilities over CRUD.** Features trace to capabilities (CAP IDs), not to database tables. A screen exists because a capability needs it.
- **Platform before product.** Identity, organizations, permissions, configuration, audit, and events were built first, with zero dairy logic. Every business module composes them; none reimplements them.
- **Configuration before customization.** Variability is data, not forks: quality dimensions are rows, not code; configuration resolves global → tenant; markets get parameters, not branches.
- **Offline-first (delivered, OFF-001).** Collection happens where connectivity fails, so the operator app captures a whole collection with no network and replays it through the online services when connectivity returns. Offline is a transport concern only — the device records, the platform decides (BR-0021).
- **Mobile-first.** The operator at 5 a.m. with a queue of farmers is the primary persona. The portal administers; the mobile app operates.
- **AI-ready.** Every capability documents its AI opportunities (CAP catalogs); the event stream and immutable transaction records are the future training substrate; `AGT` (AI agent) is a reserved architecture artifact type. No ML is deployed yet — the data discipline that enables it is.
- **Global-ready.** 50+ countries: i18n (en/sw/hi so far), IANA timezones per center, ISO-4217 currency on every rate card, and **no country-specific rules hardcoded anywhere**.
- **Event-driven.** Every business fact is published as a versioned event through the Relay. Future modules subscribe; they do not poll or reach into other modules' tables.

## 3. Engineering Philosophy

- **DDD, pragmatically.** Bounded contexts are the module folders under `platform_core/modules/`. Aggregates own invariants (e.g., a rate card owns its scope and matrices); cross-module access goes through application services and events, never through another module's tables. SQLAlchemy models double as entities — a documented pragmatism, not an accident.
- **Clean Architecture, pragmatically.** Direction of dependency: API routes → application services → models/infrastructure ports. Routers never touch the session or construct services; DI lives in `api/deps.py` (the composition root). Infrastructure is behind ports (`EventBus`, `ObjectStorage`, `Notifier`, hardware adapters) with real and in-memory/mock implementations.
- **Modular monolith.** One deployable (`services/platform-core`), many modules. Modules are the future service seams; the split criteria live in the roadmap. Do not extract services prematurely; do not couple modules so they cannot be extracted.
- **Relay / transactional outbox (SPRINT-008A).** The DI-injected `Bus` is `OutboxEventBus`: publishing writes an `event_outbox` row *inside the caller's transaction*. Rollback discards the event with the business change; commit guarantees delivery (dispatcher → CAS claim → retry with exponential backoff → dead-letter queue → replay). The event id is the message id is the idempotency key. **Never bypass it** — `get_event_bus()` is the transport, not the API.
- **Test-first mindset.** Behavior is specified by API-level tests against an in-memory stack (SQLite + in-memory bus + inline outbox — no infrastructure needed). Every work order sets a minimum test count; every bug fix lands with a regression test; the suite is green on every commit.
- **Business rules are register-defined.** The [Business Rules Register](../03-architecture/01-business-layer/BUSINESS-RULES.md) (`BR-NNNN` IDs) is the source of truth for platform invariants — BR-0001 published-card immutability, BR-0002 single active card per scope, BR-0003 exactly-one resolution, BR-0004 non-overlapping bands, and growing. New rules get a register entry, enforcing code citing the ID, and verifying tests in the same change; register↔code divergence is a defect.
- **Business objects & canonical data model.** One canonical model per concept, owned by one module (Supplier by `supplier`, RateCard by `pricing`, …). Other modules reference by id and consume events. Immutability is a first-class state: completed milk transactions, published rate cards, and active matrices are never edited — corrections are new versions or (future) adjustment transactions.
- **Concurrency by CAS.** State transitions that must not race use `UPDATE … WHERE status = <expected>` with a rowcount check (transaction accept/reject, rate card publish, outbox claim). Prefer this over `SELECT FOR UPDATE` — it is portable to the SQLite test stack.

## 4. Current Architecture

**Shape.** FastAPI modular monolith (`platform-core`) + PostgreSQL (SQLite in tests/dev-lite) + Redis + RabbitMQ + MinIO + OpenSearch, with a Next.js admin portal and a Flutter mobile app. All API under `/v1`, OpenAPI-documented, problem+json errors. **Observability (OBS-001):** every metric in one registry with enforced cardinality, four-level per-component health, 12 actionable alert rules, an operator API at `/v1/_ops/*`, and one correlation id from request through outbox and consumer to notification (BR-0024).

**Tenancy.** `Organization.id` **is** the tenant id. Tenant-scoped tokens are authoritative; platform principals act inside a tenant via `X-Tenant-ID` (permission-guarded). A contextvar carries tenant context; every query filters by it, AND **PostgreSQL row-level security enforces it in the database** — a query that forgets its filter returns nothing, so application filters are defense-in-depth (SEC-001, BR-0022).

**Identity & access.** Server-side sessions with hashed, rotating refresh tokens and reuse-as-theft revocation; access JWTs signed **RS256** from a key registry, carrying the signing `kid`, bound to a session id and revoked with it. Public verification at `/.well-known/jwks.json`; rotation is additive and invalidates nothing (SEC-001, BR-0023). RBAC: central permission registry (`modules/authz/permissions.py`, keys `<module>.<entity>.<action>`), `require_permission()` route guards, system roles (`platform-admin` = wildcard, `tenant-admin`, `tenant-viewer`) synced at startup.

Producers still know nothing about any of them.

## 6. Coding Standards

- **Naming.** Python modules/tables `snake_case`; business codes stored UPPERCASE and normalized on input (supplier `S-XXXXXX`, rate card `RC-XXXXXX`, product/dimension codes `LIKE-THIS`); permission keys `<module>.<entity>.<action>`; wire events `<domain>.<fact>.v<major>`; docs follow STD-0002/0003 (IDs like `CAP-0001`).
- **Module layout.** `models.py` (persistence + module constants) and `service.py` (application service + Pydantic DTOs: `*Command`/`*Input` in, `*View`/`*Page` out). A second service file (e.g. `pricing/matrix.py`) is acceptable when one file would become unwieldy — same module, same boundary.
- **Business objects.** Owned by exactly one module; referenced elsewhere by UUID only. Status machines are explicit verb methods with guards (`submit_for_review`, `approve`, …), not generic setters. Immutable states reject mutation with 409 and a message that names the escape hatch ("create a new version").
- **Value objects.** Realized as validated Pydantic types and normalized scalars (currency codes, QR payloads, half-open ranges) rather than ORM classes — keep validation in the DTO layer and constraints in the DB (`CheckConstraint`) when cheap.
- **Repositories.** The AsyncSession *is* the repository (documented pragmatism). Services encapsulate all queries; routers never query. If a query is needed twice, it becomes a service helper.
- **Domain vs application services.** Pure domain logic (e.g. `backoff_delay`, `_ranges_overlap`) lives as module-level functions — testable without I/O. Application services orchestrate: validate → mutate → audit → publish event, all in the request transaction.
- **Events.** Publish via the injected `Bus` only, with `aggregate_type`/`aggregate_id` set. Map PascalCase domain event names to wire names in a module-level `BUS_EVENTS` dict. Payloads carry ids and facts, not whole objects.
- **Testing.** API-level async tests via `httpx.AsyncClient`; fixtures compose real flows (org → branch → center → supplier → …) — no mocking of internal layers; the in-memory bus (`bus.published`) asserts events; SQLite + inline outbox make the suite infrastructure-free. Meet or exceed the work order's minimum; cover permissions, tenant isolation, validation failure paths, and concurrency rules (CAS) alongside happy paths.
- **Error handling.** Raise the `core/errors.py` hierarchy (`NotFoundError` 404, `ConflictError` 409 for rule violations, `ForbiddenError` 403, `InvalidTokenError` 400, …) — handlers render RFC-9457 problem+json. Never return raw 500s for anticipated conditions; never leak another tenant's existence (use 404).
- **Logging.** `structlog` key-value logging; request id + correlation id bound per request. Log facts (`event_dead_lettered`), never secrets or tokens.
- **Permissions.** Registry entry first (`permissions.py` + role lists), guard second (`require_permission`), test third (viewer-can't-manage). Startup sync (`ensure_system_roles`) picks up new keys automatically.
- **Migrations.** Alembic autogenerate against a scratch DB, then verify `upgrade → downgrade → upgrade`, then lint. Never edit an applied migration; chain is linear.
- **Comments.** Only for constraints the code cannot express (walls like "no calculation here", concurrency reasoning, documented pragmatisms). No narration.
- **Datetimes.** Timezone-aware UTC everywhere; use `as_utc()` before comparing anything read from SQLite; `utcnow()` from `core/db.py`.

## 7. Completed Work (the platform's evolution)

4. **SPRINT-001 Bootstrap.** One-command dev environment; portal and mobile scaffolds with live status screens; Makefile; CI for all three codebases; committed Alembic baseline.
5. **SPRINT-002 Identity & Organization.** Real session security (rotation, theft detection), workspaces/branches/membership/invitations, RBAC wired end-to-end.
6. **SPRINT-003 Collection Centers.** Facility management only: lifecycle, config, operating hours, calendar; first full-stack business slices in portal + mobile.
7. **SPRINT-004 Operational Readiness.** Device registry/assignment/health, operator assignment, and the readiness engine gating operations — mock adapters only.
8. **SPRINT-005 Supplier Platform.** Supplier lifecycle, profiles, documents, banking, m:n center placement, signed QR identity, search, resilient bulk import.
9. **SPRINT-007 Milk Collection Engine.** Readiness-gated sessions; the immutable transaction state machine with snapshot/event-log/metrics; QR/code/phone/manual identification; mock scale/analyzer; pricing left as an explicit placeholder. *(SPRINT-006 was never issued — the numbering gap is historical, not lost work.)*
10. **SPRINT-008A Relay.** The transactional outbox retrofitted *behind* the existing bus port — ~40 publish sites became durable with zero call-site changes; CAS dispatch, retries, DLQ, replay, relay ops API.
11. **Pricing Increment-001 Rate Card Foundation.** The rate card aggregate: five-status review workflow, immutable-once-published, versioning with copied scope, center+product assignment, effective-date overlap rule (one published card per center+product+date).
12. **Pricing Increment-002 Pricing Matrix Foundation.** Configurable quality dimensions as tenant data (FAT is not hardcoded); per product×dimension matrices of half-open `[from, to)` price bands with overlap rejection, dimension bounds, continuity gap reporting; lifecycle riding the rate card (draft-editable → active on publish → archived with card → copied by new versions). **Definitions only — no calculation exists anywhere.**
13. **PRC-003 Pricing Resolution Engine.** Read-side selection: (center, product, date, dimension, reading) → exactly one published card → one active matrix → one band, via a fixed 3-query pipeline with an explicit reusable repository; structured `pricing_no_match` (stage/reason/inputs) and `pricing_integrity` (candidates) exceptions — never a silent choice; platform `Money`/`Quantity` value objects introduced (arithmetic-free until PRC-004). Portal playground + mobile test screen.
14. **PRC-004 Pricing Calculator.** The first monetary calculation: pure deterministic domain service computing gross = unit price × quantity in Decimal only (float factors rejected by type guard), configurable rounding (HALF_UP default / HALF_EVEN / DOWN; request → tenant config → default), complete 4-step trace with exact raw amounts, server-side re-verification of the resolved band (clients send row ids, never prices), `pricing.calculated.v1` through the outbox; stateless — the event is the record. Rules BR-0005…0007 catalogued.
15. **SET-001 Settlement Foundation.** The `settlement` module: supplier+center+period payables whose lines are built from the durable calculation events (via `RelayService.find_aggregate_event` — amounts never client-supplied), exact `Money.plus` totals with a finalize-time integrity gate, draft→calculated→finalized (CAS, immutable) plus history-preserving cancel that releases calculations and periods; supplier-wide period-overlap ban; Numeric money columns (first schema under the new precision policy); BR-0008…0012. No payment (SET-002).
16. **MVP-001 End-to-End Procurement Integration.** No new modules — the seams closed: milk transactions invoke resolution (FAT) + calculator at their pricing step (never blocking collection; `pricing_unavailable` degrades gracefully) and persist verified amounts + `calculation_id`; settlements bulk-collect eligible period transactions idempotently; the `awaiting_pricing_engine` placeholder is retired. Portal global nav + procurement dashboard; wizard shows payable amounts. `test_procurement_e2e.py` proves the whole journey including the event chain and audit trail.
17. **SPRINT-008B Event Consumer Framework.** The Relay's receiving half: durable-log consumers (cursor + idempotency ledger + ordering + backoff/DLQ/replay), total producer isolation (BR-0013/0014), auto-discovered independent consumers package, `/_consumers` ops API. Engineering review: `require_current_tenant()` and `envelope_from_outbox()` dedup. *(Delivered after REP-001.)*
18. **REP-001 Reporting Foundation.** Read-only `reporting` module: SQL aggregation over live transactional data (documented boundary exception — owns nothing, writes nothing), five `/v1/reports` endpoints (daily, by-center, by-supplier, settlements, pricing) with weighted quality averages and fixed query budgets; portal /reports section; mobile "Today" center summary. *(Delivered before SPRINT-008B.)*
19. **PLT-001 Projection Lifecycle & Replay.** Projections (a `Projection` IS a consumer) gained metadata, a derived-status registry, a log-only rebuild engine (batched, dry-run, progress/ETA, cancellable), reset, versioning, and integrity verification incl. rolled-back shadow-replay drift detection — BR-0015: every projection is fully rebuildable from the event log. See [Projection Lifecycle & Replay](../03-architecture/04-technology-layer/PROJECTION-LIFECYCLE.md).
20. **NOT-001 Notification Engine (Phase A).** Messaging became a consumer of the event log instead of something modules call: producers hold no notifier and instead emit events carrying what a message would need, `notification-dispatch` maps 8 events onto templates, and a rebuildable recipient-directory projection supplies phone/email/locale so dispatch never calls back into a business module (BR-0016). Every message renders from a `(key, channel, language)` template — a missing variable is an error, not a half-substituted SMS — and delivery is idempotent on (event, template, channel) with the notification (not the event) owning its retry budget (BR-0017). Providers are adapters only: logging and placeholder, **no SES/Twilio/credentials**. Framework fix: registered consumers now run projections first. See [Notification Engine](../03-architecture/04-technology-layer/NOTIFICATION-ENGINE.md).
21. **PAY-001 Payment Execution Engine.** The `payment` module: payments consume FINALIZED settlements and never modify them, allocating per settlement so partial payment, remaining balance, and duplicate prevention fall out of one rule — outstanding = payable − live allocations, where a draft already reserves and failure/cancellation release (BR-0018). Lifecycle draft→pending→processing→completed (immutable) with retryable failure and terminal cancellation, every transition CAS-guarded, every execution a new attempt row so failure history survives success (BR-0019). Idempotency key prevents double payment; methods (bank/cash/cheque/mobile money) are metadata — no gateway, no credentials. Six outbox events; `payment.completed.v1` gives the NOT-001 notification template its producer.22. **RCP-001 Receipt Engine.** The `receipt` module: receipts are generated ONLY by consuming `payment.completed.v1`, one per completed payment (unique `(tenant, payment)`, so replay re-finds rather than re-mints), and their content is COPIED at generation because a receipt must show the world as it was when the money moved (BR-0020). Lifecycle generated→delivered→archived is the only permitted mutation; archived stays queryable; there is no update or delete path. Rendering is a pure derivation of the frozen record — nothing stored, any format re-derivable identically — through a `ReceiptRenderer` registry mirroring the NOT-001 provider abstraction: JSON, self-contained HTML, and a **PDF placeholder that announces itself**; no PDF engine ships. `payment.completed.v1` was enriched so the consumer never calls back into another module. See [Receipt Rendering](../03-architecture/04-technology-layer/RECEIPT-RENDERING.md).23. **OFF-001 Offline Collection Sync.** The operator app collects with no connectivity and reconciles when it returns, without forking a rule: a durable Flutter queue (atomic file store, PENDING→SYNCING→SYNCED/FAILED/CONFLICT, backoff, cancellation, resume) feeds a backend `sync` module that replays every operation through the ONLINE collection service. Idempotent on a client-generated operation id; local ids resolved to server ids across batches and persisted server-side, so an interrupted sync resumes. Conflicts are structured and surfaced, never silently overwritten — including `rate_card_changed`, which applies anyway because milk is perishable. The device records; the platform decides (BR-0021). See [Offline Collection Sync](../03-architecture/04-technology-layer/OFFLINE-SYNC.md).24. **SEC-001 Production Security Hardening (Phase B).** No business behaviour changed; the trust boundaries did. RS256 tokens from a rotatable key registry with `kid` resolution (no algorithm-from-header, no key fallback), JWKS discovery, additive rotation and immediate retirement; PostgreSQL RLS on every tenant-owned table with `FORCE` + `WITH CHECK`, failing closed when unbound, with an explicit logged bypass for cross-tenant machinery; security headers and a narrowed CORS allow-list; Redis rate limiting on credential and expensive endpoints, keyed per-IP and per-identifier, failing open by choice; production refuses to start on a development credential; and a security audit vocabulary mirrored to the log stream. New `docs/03-architecture/05-security/` layer with threat model, rotation runbook, RLS guide, and incident-response checklist.25. **OBS-001 Platform Observability & Alerting (Phase B).** Operators can now see platform, consumer, projection, notification, payment, and security health without reading logs. Built for this platform's real failure shape — event-driven work can stop while the API keeps answering — so background workers are a health component, consumer lag is a headline metric, and alerts name the business consequence. All 50 metrics centralized with a test-enforced cardinality rule; correlation ids rebound by consumers so a chain crossing a process boundary stays joinable (BR-0024); four-level health where the worst component wins and degraded stays ready; 12 alert rules that each carry an action; consumer pause/resume; OTel supported but optional. 7 generated Grafana dashboards and Prometheus rules generated from the platform's own definitions. See [OBSERVABILITY](../03-architecture/06-operations/OBSERVABILITY.md).26. **BAK-001 Backup, Restore & Disaster Recovery (Phase B).** The platform can prove it recovers: a test builds a real dairy, destroys every row, restores, and asserts the money, the evidence, and every business invariant survived — including a deep projection rebuild (BR-0025). Tables are classified critical/important/rebuildable/ephemeral from the metadata; projections are excluded (BR-0015 rebuilds them) and consumer ledgers deliberately included, because restoring without them re-sends every notification. Deterministic checksums, type-preserving serialization, corruption detected before a restore depends on it, and restore is CLI-only with a non-zero exit when the restored business is wrong. **Found a live latent bug:** `migrations/env.py` had lost every model import to a `ruff --fix`, so the next autogenerate would have dropped every table; restored, linter-exempted, and guarded by `test_migrations.py`. See [BACKUP](../03-architecture/06-operations/BACKUP.md) and [RESTORE](../03-architecture/06-operations/RESTORE.md).27. **CI-001 PostgreSQL Proof.** Infrastructure and verification only — no business behaviour or API changed. Every PostgreSQL-dependent guarantee moved from *documented* to *proven* by one script (`infra/ci/postgres-proof.sh`) that CI and `docker-compose.proof.yml` both run: migrations applied to an EMPTY database, RLS enabled **and forced** on every tenant table, the RLS suite passing and provably not skipped, a real dairy seeded through the platform's own API, a logical backup taken and verified, a **second fresh database** migrated, the backup restored into it, deep business integrity checked, and source vs restored compared fact by fact. Running it found three defects every existing test had passed over: the RLS predicate hid every platform-global row because `NULL = 'x'` is NULL — **registration itself would have failed** in production (fixed by migration `c94b1ea27f31`); a CLI backup captured **one table and three rows** while reporting success; and restore crashed ordering a table it could not see. The last two shared a root cause now closed by `core/model_registry.py` — `Base.metadata` completeness is an import-order property, so `import_all_models()` is a **call** no linter can delete (BR-0026). See [POSTGRES-PROOF](../03-architecture/06-operations/POSTGRES-PROOF.md).28. **DBR-001 Database Inventory & Review.** Documentation only — nothing changed. [DBD-0001](../07-data/DBD-0001-platform-core-database.md) is now the permanent Database Architecture Reference: 56 tables by module with full column/key/constraint/index detail, four ERDs, a module dependency graph from real imports, hot-warm-cold lifecycle, a stated scale model, the top-20 tables by volume, a partitioning assessment, and 13 numbered findings. Every structural fact is generated from `Base.metadata`, not typed. The findings that matter: **19 of 56 tables have no `tenant_id` and no RLS** (15 of them have no FK either, including `supplier_profile` and `supplier_bank_account`), so BR-0022 covers 37 tables not 56; **4 foreign keys and 2 check constraints exist in the whole schema**; **`pricing_matrix_row.unit_price` is a `FLOAT`**, so BR-0005's exact arithmetic starts from an inexact input; and there is **no retention, archival, or tenant-deletion path** against ~12 TB/year of growth.29. **SEC-002 Complete PostgreSQL RLS Coverage.** *Every table has an explicit isolation strategy* stops being a principle and becomes a build check: 50 tenant-owned (derived from metadata, never listed), 5 platform-global (each with its reason recorded in `core/rls.py`), `organization` isolated by its own primary key, **0 undeclared**. Thirteen tenant-owned child tables — including `supplier_profile` and `settlement_line` — gained `tenant_id` and a policy; they had been reachable only through a parent, so BR-0022's promise was false for them. **Verifying the flows found a worse defect: authentication itself was broken under RLS.** Principal resolution read `auth_session` and `user_account` (both tenant-owned) *before* binding the token's tenant, so every authenticated request without an `X-Tenant-ID` header would have failed 401. Binding now happens from the signed token claim before the first read; tenant-scoped login, invitation acceptance, and organization creation were repaired the same way, and `rebind_tenant()` moves the context variable and the database binding together. See [RLS-GUIDE](../03-architecture/05-security/RLS-GUIDE.md) §3b–3c.30. **DB-002 Exact Numeric Aggregation.** No API response changed; the reporting module was not redesigned. Money arithmetic was already exact (BR-0005) but **aggregation was not**: `SUM(net_weight)` over `double precision` is order-dependent, and the result was stored in a `NUMERIC(16,3)` column, which made a non-reproducible number look exact. Every aggregation was classified — 13 converted (all in `reporting/service.py`), 1 accepted as lossy (`AVG(latency_ms)`), the rest already safe over `NUMERIC` or integers. `_exact()` casts to unconstrained `NUMERIC` inside the aggregate; the arithmetic stays in SQL. **A second divergence surfaced while testing:** the reporting projection rounded whenever a flush occurred — per event incrementally, per BATCH on rebuild — so a rebuild did not reproduce the incremental total (0.006 vs 0.003 for six weights of 0.0005 kg). That was a hole in BR-0015; the handler now quantizes to the column's scale at every step. BR-0005 and BR-0015 both extended (register v1.14).31. **OPS-001 PostgreSQL Continuous Verification.** Verification infrastructure only. The PostgreSQL-only guarantees — RLS, exact aggregation, migrations-from-empty, verified restore — moved into their own workflow (`.github/workflows/postgres.yml`) with a **version matrix** (16 production baseline, 17 latest validated; 18 deliberately absent until something has run it), a **nightly schedule** because a dependency bump can break a guarantee without anyone touching the repo, and a single `PostgreSQL verified` check for branch protection. **Skips became impossible rather than detected:** `tests/postgres_support.py` raises at collection when `LACTEVA_REQUIRE_POSTGRES=1` and no database URL is set, and the proof script re-asserts the counts from `--junitxml` rather than from pytest's prose. The script also gained a published step summary, real teardown (`KEEP_DATABASES=1` to inspect a failure), and a missing-report guard. `ci.yml` keeps the fast SQLite pipeline unchanged; the duplicated job was removed rather than left to drift.32. **DEP-001 Production Deployment Automation.** No business behaviour, API or schema changed. A nine-container production stack with one published port (nginx); startup ordering by health check with **no sleep anywhere**; a one-shot `migrate` service that must exit zero before the API starts, so a failed migration aborts the deployment rather than serving against a schema that did not apply. **Two operational defects fixed:** background loops were `cancel()`ed mid-transaction (now cooperative — each finishes its unit of work, stragglers cancelled after a grace, engine disposed last), and `/health/ready` was a `SELECT 1` that told a load balancer an instance was ready while its consumer loop was dead (now the same nine probes the alerts read; degraded stays ready; answered from the sampler, not re-probed per poll). Plus `.env.production.example` with every variable documented, Docker Secrets via `secrets_dir`, production nginx, Loki+Promtail, `verify-deployment.sh` (including RLS coverage), and `smoke-test.py`. See [DEPLOYMENT.md](../../DEPLOYMENT.md) — the rollback compatibility matrix is the part to read before an upgrade.33. **INF-001 Infrastructure as Code & Production Environment.** Infrastructure only. Terraform for Hetzner (primary) and AWS (thin optional abstraction), provisioning one VM with a **static IP and data volume that outlive the machine** — that pairing is the whole server-replacement story. Both configurations **refuse `0.0.0.0/0` for SSH at plan time**; only 80/443/SSH are reachable; `prevent_destroy` on the IP and volume; Terraform never formats (cloud-init does, and only when `blkid` finds no filesystem). cloud-init installs Docker, builds the filesystem standard, hardens SSH, brings up ufw + fail2ban as a second layer, and configures chrony — not cosmetic, since a drifting clock rejects every JWT and looks like a key problem. systemd starts on boot with `RequiresMountsFor` and a stop timeout exceeding the API's drain. Backup timers: nightly logical (verify, **then** prune), weekly physical, weekly restore verification (BR-0025). `deploy.sh` is pull → backup → migrate → up → verify → smoke with automatic rollback, never automatic schema rollback. See [INFRASTRUCTURE.md](../../INFRASTRUCTURE.md).34. **MT-001 Full Multi-Tenancy Audit.** Three defects. **The bypass was documented and never implemented** — the relay, consumers, projection rebuilds, backup and health probes built sessions from the ordinary factory, so under RLS they saw only platform-global rows: nothing dispatched, nothing consumed, every projection write refused, and no error anywhere. Fixed with `PlatformSessionFactory`, so a cross-tenant component *cannot* forget and a request-scoped one is visibly different. **The per-identifier rate-limit key was not tenant-scoped** — email is unique per tenant, so one tenant's failed logins spent another tenant's budget for the same address (cross-tenant DoS on authentication). **The notification dispatcher resolved recipients by `subject_id` alone**, inside the bypass where RLS is off by design — the filter was all that stood between a payment notification and another dairy's phone. Reviewed-and-correct: object keys, document download, receipts, pagination totals, reporting joins, audit, JWT authority, backup. No websockets exist and a test keeps it that way. BR-0022 extended (register v1.15); [RLS-GUIDE §3d](../03-architecture/05-security/RLS-GUIDE.md) is the boundary rule.35. **API-001 Complete REST API Review.** All 177 operations reviewed as a public SaaS surface. **The error contract was never published** — the platform always returned RFC 9457 problem details and OpenAPI declared only FastAPI's 422, so every generated client saw an unknown error shape. Now published centrally by three mechanical rules (universal 401/403/422/429; 404 where there is a path parameter; 409 where the operation mutates). **Page sizes were clamped silently**, so a client paginating by 1000 read 100 and skipped 900 — bounds are now declared on the route and violations get 422 rather than being quietly honoured as something else. **`/v1/collection-sessions` had no bound at all** and is now paginated. Added the conventional `Idempotency-Key` header on payment creation. Reviewed-and-correct: REST semantics, CAS-guarded transitions (retry-safe by construction), 404-not-403 for other tenants, token authority over the header, audit coverage, presigned downloads. See [API-0001](../06-api/API-0001-platform-core.md) — including seven recorded limitations, of which no general idempotency and no `ETag` matter most.36. **IDM-001 Platform-wide Idempotency.** `Idempotency-Key` on every POST/PUT/PATCH, not a payment-specific solution. **The reservation, the business write and the stored response share one transaction** — a router-level dependency has the session but not the response; a route class has the response and, because FastAPI closes teardown *after* the handler returns, still has the session. That ordering was probed before committing to the design and is pinned by a test. A retry after completion replays the original response verbatim; in-flight gets 409; a different body under the same key gets 400; a failed operation releases its key. **Two defects found while building it:** `include_router` preserves each child's route class, so declaring it on the parent alone wrapped nothing (idempotency would have looked enabled while storing nothing); and router-level dependencies run before authentication, so keys were reserved with a NULL tenant — every tenant in one namespace, the same class of defect MT-001 found in the rate limiter. Retention 24 h, swept by a background worker. See [API-0001 §5](../06-api/API-0001-platform-core.md).37. **MSG-001 Production SMS Delivery.** Suppliers can now actually be told — the platform rendered, dispatched, retried and recorded every message, then handed it to a provider that discarded it. **The central finding was the retry engine, not the adapter:** every failure was retried, so an invalid phone number spent five gateway calls and five backoff windows reaching the answer it had immediately, and a missing template (a deployment fault) did the same. Providers now claim permanence and the engine honours it; the base error stays retryable because an unfamiliar failure must not silently drop a supplier's message. Vendor-neutral `HttpSmsProvider` classifies by HTTP status; every send carries a stable `Idempotency-Key` because only the gateway knows it already accepted a message whose response we lost. Five modes including **`dry_run`** (real message, production config, not sent — staging) and **`disabled`** (raises rather than silently succeeding). `mask_phone()` at every log site. BR-0017 extended (register v1.16). See [NOTIFICATION-ENGINE](../03-architecture/04-technology-layer/NOTIFICATION-ENGINE.md).38. **ARCH-001 Final Production Readiness Review.** Independent pass over every subsystem. **Four fixes, one critical: two concurrent payments could each claim the full outstanding balance of one settlement** — an unlocked read-modify-write that READ COMMITTED does not protect, with no unique constraint able to catch it because partial payment is legitimate. The settlement is now locked `FOR UPDATE` before the balance is read. Also: **the connection pool was still unconfigured** four work orders after being rated the highest-probability first incident (no pre-ping, no statement or lock timeout) — now set, with background sessions raised to 15 minutes rather than exempted; a missing `(tenant_id, status)` index on the payables selector; and `amount > 0` on payment, in a schema that had two check constraints. Migration `ced805436869`. See CHANGELOG for the full finding list.

Current test posture: **845 backend tests — 792 on SQLite (fast, hermetic, every push) and 30 PostgreSQL-only (32 RLS + 6 exact aggregation), which cannot skip in the verification workflow** — plus 49 Flutter tests, portal build+lint, and docs validator + XREF. Two CI workflows: `ci.yml` (SQLite, docs, portal, mobile, image) and `postgres.yml` (the nine-step proof on a 16/17 matrix, nightly and on every change). Schema posture: **56 tables**, 21 linear migrations, 4 foreign keys, and **RLS on every table that has a tenant** — see [DBD-0001](../07-data/DBD-0001-platform-core-database.md).

## 9. What NOT To Do

1. **Do not redesign the architecture.** The baseline is locked; work orders explicitly forbid it. Extend within the established shapes.
2. **Do not bypass Relay.** All business events go through the injected `Bus` (the outbox). Never call `get_event_bus()` from module code; never publish after commit.
3. **Do not violate DDD boundaries.** Never query another module's tables; go through its service or consume its events. Never put business logic in routers.
4. **Do not duplicate business logic.** One owner per concept. Reuse the CAS pattern, the status-machine pattern, the search/pagination pattern, the QR pattern — do not reinvent them per module.
5. **Do not introduce cloud dependencies unnecessarily.** Local runs on Docker Compose (or SQLite-lite without Docker); tests need no infrastructure at all. AWS enters via `infra/` at M3, behind ports that already exist.
6. **Do not hardcode country- or market-specific rules.** No currency defaults in logic, no locale assumptions, no regulatory constants. Variability is configuration/business data (see: quality dimensions).
7. **Do not create tightly coupled modules.** A module must remain extractable: id references and events only.
8. **Do not mutate the immutable.** Completed transactions, published rate cards, active/archived matrices, historical versions, applied migrations, the outbox audit trail — corrections are new versions, adjustments, or replays.
9. **Do not add permissions, events, or config keys ad hoc.** Registry first, code second — that is what keeps portals, roles, and docs enumerable.
10. **Do not weaken tests to pass.** Fix the code or (with justification recorded) the spec. Never delete history, never force-push, never commit local artifacts (`dev.db`, `.env` — gitignored for a reason).
